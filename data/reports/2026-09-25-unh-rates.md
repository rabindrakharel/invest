# UNH in a high-rate environment, 2026-09-25
As of: prices 2026-09-24 close · runway record 2026-09-24 · macro 2026-09-25 run (series to 09-24) · Finviz snapshot 2026-09-24
Regime: Lean risk-off (composite -4.8), duration hostile · 10y 5.11%, real 10y 2.76% · risk budget 60% gross

## Answer
Yes. UNH survives high rates comfortably, because rates are not what threatens it. It isn't a long-duration asset: at $375 it trades at 14.3× free cash flow, about a 7% FCF yield, which clears a 5.11% 10-year by about 2 points. Its $23.6B of trailing FCF covers the dividend about 2.9× and implied net debt of about $45B in about 2 years. As an insurer it also collects premiums before it pays claims, so higher rates, if anything, raise its investment income. The real risks are **medical-cost inflation** (which this oil-shock, hiking-cycle regime feeds), **Medicare Advantage policy** into the 3 Nov midterms, and thin margins (operating margin 4.8%). "Survive" also doesn't mean "lead." In a strong-growth, rising-yield tape the market sells health care as a defensive, and UNH is -18.9% vs SPY over 3 months. The probe verdict stands: half position now, with the rest earned or killed at the **13 Oct** print.

## Why
- **Valuation clears the rate hurdle.** P/FCF 14.25, so FCF yield ≈ 7.0%, vs the 10y at 5.11% and the real yield at 2.76%. Forward P/E is 17.6, beta 0.61. The rate penalty in this regime falls on assets whose value sits years out (long_duration_growth fit -1.3); UNH earns it now (`records/UNH.json`; Finviz 2026-09-24).
- **Balance sheet and cash:**
  - Debt/equity 0.74 and current ratio 0.78, normal for an insurer.
  - Implied net debt ≈ $44.7B (EV $381.35B minus market cap $336.61B), about 1.9 years of FCF.
  - FCF of $23.6B is 1.67× net income ($14.1B).
  - The dividend of $9.06 (2.4% yield, 66% payout of GAAP EPS) costs about $8.2B, covered about 2.9× by FCF.
  - Refinancing at 5% raises interest cost at the margin, but nothing here strains solvency.
- **Rates can help an insurer.** Premiums arrive before claims are paid, and that float is invested. The playbook lists insurers among the beneficiaries of real yields up and Fed hiking (`playbook.md` §3). The size of UNH's investment income was not fetched, so the benefit's magnitude is unmeasured.
- **Inflation is the real threat, not rates.** Premiums reprice once a year, while medical costs move continuously. A cost-trend surprise, not a rate move, is what broke the stock in the 2025 reset. Quarterly operating margin swung 3.81% → 2.46% → 8.05% → 7.13% (Q3-25 to Q2-26). The regime has an oil shock (WTI $93, +34% in 3 months) and a hiking cycle, both of which push service inflation. So this is the risk to watch.
- **Earnings momentum is on its side for now:**
  - FY26 guide raised to $19.50–20.00; consensus EPS $19.87, up from $18.39 90 days ago.
  - FY27 EPS about $22.52 (+13.7%).
  - Q2 beat: $6.38 vs $4.94.
  - The red-team caveat stands: the last 30 days moved only +0.3%.

## What would change it
- **2026-10-13, UNH Q3:** a medical care ratio above guidance, or FY26 held or cut below the $19.75 midpoint, is the kill. A raise completes the position.
- **2026-10-14 CPI:** core services running hot is the channel that reaches medical costs.
- **2026-11-03 midterms:** a Democratic House (about 87% odds per the news file) puts Medicare Advantage rates and PBM reform back in play. That is policy risk, not rate risk.
