# Doctrine: how to perceive each data point, and how to reach a verdict

Read this before every probe. It is the difference between a scorecard and an analysis.
The scorecard says how many points a signal earns. The doctrine says what the signal
means, what it does not mean, what usually follows it, and how it interacts with the
others. The verdict comes from the interaction, never from the sum.

## 1. The stance

- **You are estimating a distribution, not picking a winner.** Every name gets a
  bull, base and bear price twelve months out with probabilities that sum to one. The
  expected return and the downside in the bear case are what the mandate cares about.
- **Prices already contain the consensus.** A fact everyone can read on Finviz is in
  the price. Edge comes from disagreement with consensus that the evidence supports,
  from timing, or from a signal the market discounts wrongly (insider clusters, quiet
  names with rising estimates). Say which kind of edge each Runway name has. A name
  with no stated edge is a beta bet.
- **Base rates before stories.** Every signal below carries what usually happens.
  Start from that and let the specifics move you. A story that needs the base rate to
  be wrong needs proportionally stronger evidence.
- **Evidence has weight by cost.** What someone paid for outranks what someone said.
  A CEO's $9M open-market buy outranks a broker's target. A guide raise the company will
  be held to outranks a bullish transcript quote. A 13F filing outranks a fund manager's
  interview. Corpus commentary is the cheapest evidence of all; use it for positioning,
  never for facts.
- **Falsifiability is the test of a thesis.** Every verdict states what would prove it
  wrong, with a number and a date. A thesis with no kill criterion is not a thesis.

## 2. Perceiving each data point

For each signal: what it is, what it means, what it does not mean, the base rate, the
traps, and the second-order read.

### Earnings print and guidance

- **Means:** whether the business is doing what the price assumes. The guide matters
  more than the quarter; management is setting the bar it will be judged against.
- **Does not mean:** a beat is not good news if the stock had run into it, and a miss is
  not bad news if it was pre-announced or the guide was raised anyway.
- **Base rate:** after a large surprise, price drifts in the same direction for roughly
  two to three months. A beat-and-raise met with a sell-off is the exception worth
  studying: it usually means positioning was crowded or the guide's quality was poor.
- **Traps:** revenue beats driven by one-offs; EPS beats from buybacks or tax; guides
  raised by less than the beat (a "beat and hold" dressed as a raise); adjusted
  metrics that exclude recurring costs.
- **Second-order read:** compare the print reaction with the revision direction two
  weeks later. Price down and estimates up is the setup where the market is wrong.
  Price up and estimates down is the setup where the market is early to leave.

### Estimate revisions

- **Means:** the sell-side's aggregate updating. Up-revisions persist and are the most
  robust cross-sectional predictor of forward returns after momentum.
- **Does not mean:** the level of consensus is right; only its direction has
  information.
- **Trap:** revisions mechanically follow the print for a week, so read them at least
  ten days after it.

### Free cash flow, net income, stock-based compensation

- **Means:** whether earnings are cash. Conversion below 70% with receivables rising
  is the classic quality flag.
- **Does not mean:** negative FCF is disqualifying; a company in a proven investment
  phase (visible backlog, contracted returns) is spending, not burning. Distinguish the
  two by whether the returns are already contracted.
- **Trap:** SBC above 10% of revenue is dilution the adjusted EPS hides. Count it.

### Prospect

- **Means:** the twelve-month causal chain from today to the target. State it as one
  sentence with the number it depends on.
- **Perception rule:** rank by visibility. Reported beats guided beats story. The
  price of a story name is a claim on a future you cannot audit.
- **Trap:** total addressable market arguments. TAM says nothing about share, price or
  timing. A prospect that rests on TAM alone scores 1.

### Competition and moat

- **Means:** whether the returns the prospect needs will be competed away. Gross
  margin trend is the cleanest single reading of moat.
- **Reads:** margin up with share stable is pricing power; margin down with share up is
  buying growth; margin down with share down is losing. A named entrant with a shipping
  product is a fact; a rumoured one is not.
- **Trap:** relative valuation against peers that are themselves mispriced. Anchor on
  the peer with the most similar growth and margin, not the sector average.

### Insider buying and selling

- **Means:** what the people with the best information do with their own money.
- **Base rate:** open-market buys by officers outperform the market over the following
  year by a few percent on average; clusters of three or more within a month, buys
  after a 30% or larger drawdown, and a CEO's first buy in years are the strong forms.
  Individual sales carry almost no information; clustered sales and a CEO selling more
  than a year's pay in a month do.
- **Does not mean:** insiders know the quarter. They know the business and the price
  they think is cheap; they are often early by six months or more.
- **Traps:** buys that are a small fraction of the insider's holdings or salary; buys
  timed to a public announcement; 10b5-1 sells read as conviction sells; option
  exercises counted as buys.
- **Second-order read:** an insider cluster with negative estimate revisions is the
  most interesting combination in the set. Either the insiders see the trough before
  the analysts, or the drawdown has a cause the filings do not show. Find the cause
  before sizing.

### Institutional flow (13F)

- **Means:** who owned what at quarter end, filed 45 days later. It is a lagging
  positioning map, not a flow gauge.
- **Reads:** rising new-holder count with a concentrated hedge-fund add is the
  positive form; index funds cutting is passive and says nothing; a large named exit
  by a fund known for the sector is a signal to understand.
- **Does not mean:** funds are buying now. By the time you read it the position may be
  gone.
- **Traps:** aggregator errors (the CalSTRS row); filer-entity changes reported as
  new positions; dollar totals swollen by price moves rather than share changes.
- **Second-order read:** short interest above 20% of float with a passing delivery
  gate is a squeeze setup, and the reverse is a name the shorts may be right about.

### Retail interest (corpus)

- **Means:** how much of the audience that follows these accounts has already been
  told to buy. It is a crowding gauge.
- **Reads:** loud, multiple high-conviction longs and price near the high means the
  marginal buyer has been reached; loud after a 40% drawdown is a capitulation watch
  and can mark a low; quiet with rising estimates means the audience has not arrived.
- **Does not mean:** the accounts are wrong. Their large-cap 200-week-average entries
  coincided with real recoveries. Use them for timing, not direction.
- **Trap:** engagement counts are capture-time snapshots with different ages; compare
  ratios, not totals.

### Price, drawdown and upside to consensus

- **Means:** where the market is relative to the analysts. A large gap to consensus
  after a drawdown usually means the targets have not been cut yet, not that the
  stock is cheap.
- **Reads:** compare the average target's history: a target that fell 30% and still
  shows 50% upside is a falling knife with a lagging label. A target that rose after
  the print with the stock flat is the setup that works.
- **Trap:** the average of targets set before and after a guidance change.

### Risk : reward

- **Means:** how much can be made against how much can be lost to the nearest place
  buyers showed up before.
- **Rule:** the anchor is the highest of the low target, the 52-week low, and a
  well-defined base or the 200-day line if price is above it. If the anchor is more than
  40% below, the reward is being bought with a very deep stop; size accordingly.
- **Does not mean:** R : R substitutes for probability. A 4 : 1 with a 20% chance of
  the bull case is worse than a 2 : 1 with a 60% chance. The scenario tree resolves
  this.

### Daily chart

- **Means:** what the aggregate of all participants has done with the information so
  far. Trend persists over three to twelve months; the 200-day line is the reference
  most participants watch, and the first reclaim after a long time below it draws
  buyers who were waiting for exactly that.
- **Reads:** print gap held for five sessions confirms the print; gap filled denies it.
  Relative strength against the sector separates the company from its group. A base
  (a range under 15% for six weeks or more) after a large drawdown is where insiders
  and 13F adds are worth the most, because supply has been absorbed.
- **Does not mean:** a chart predicts; it describes. A broken chart with improving
  estimates and insider buying is a disagreement, and disagreements are where returns
  are.
- **Trap:** reading moving-average distances from an aggregator as if they were a
  price series. Say what was actually seen.

### Macro climate

- **Means:** the discount rate and the risk appetite every name is priced against.
- **Reads:** long-duration equities need falling real yields; cash-flow equities
  tolerate a plateau; rising 10-year with widening high-yield spreads hits small caps
  and levered balance sheets first; a strong dollar hits EM and exporters; oil above
  $85 favours energy and hurts consumer; when a single-A corporate bond yields 6%, an
  equity without a visible free-cash-flow yield or an earnings inflection is the wrong
  shape.
- **Does not mean:** macro decides single names. It decides which shapes of name the
  regime rewards and what the hurdle is. Say the hurdle in one number.

### Narrative harmony

- **Means:** whether the market's current story is a tailwind. Names inside an
  accelerating theme get multiple expansion on top of earnings; names inside a fading
  theme de-rate on good prints.
- **Reads:** a theme is accelerating while its counter-narrative is losing evidence.
  Track the counter-narrative explicitly (for AI capex: return on capex; for GLP-1:
  compounded competition and pricing; for AI power: hyperscaler capex pause). When
  the counter-narrative starts winning, every name in the theme is exposed at once,
  which is a correlation the mandate must count.
- **Trap:** a name that is only a narrative. If the prospect scores 1 and the
  narrative scores 7, the name is a story with a tailwind, and it belongs in a sleeve
  with a kill criterion, never in core.

### Catalysts

- **Means:** dated resolutions. A binary event inside three months turns a position
  into a bet with two outcomes; the mandate's core cannot hold that.
- **Read:** the catalyst that would confirm the thesis and the one that would kill it.
  Both go into the verdict.

## 3. Combining: the interaction table

Read the signals in pairs before scoring the whole. These pairs carry most of the
information:

| Pair | Read |
|---|---|
| Estimates up, price down after the print | market wrong or positioning was crowded; the best entry setup in the set |
| Insider cluster, estimates down | insiders early or a hidden cause; find the cause, then it is the second-best setup |
| 13F adds, short interest above 20% | squeeze fuel with sponsorship; violent either way |
| Loud in the corpus, near the high, insiders selling | distribution; the audience is the exit |
| Quiet in the corpus, guide raised, chart reclaiming the 200 | the audience has not arrived; sponsorship will follow |
| Theme accelerating, prospect a story | multiple expansion without earnings; sleeve only, with a kill date |
| Theme fading, delivery gate passes | de-rating on good prints; wait for the counter-narrative to exhaust |
| Deep upside to consensus, targets falling | lagging label, not value; wait for the target trajectory to turn |
| Macro headwind, long duration, no FCF | wrong shape for the regime regardless of story |
| Cash-flow equity, macro plateau, R : R above 2 | the regime's preferred shape; the core candidate |

## 4. The verdict procedure

For every Runway and One-leg-missing name:

1. **State the edge** in one sentence: disagreement with consensus, timing, or a
   discounted signal. If none, write "beta".
2. **Build the scenario tree.** Bull, base and bear price twelve months out with a
   probability each, each tied to a concrete condition (the guide hits, the PPA lands,
   the counter-narrative wins). Expected return = sum of probability times return.
   Bear-case loss is the number the mandate reads.
3. **Compare to the hurdle** from the macro brief. An expected return below the hurdle
   plus a risk premium appropriate to the name's volatility is a pass regardless of
   score.
4. **Write the kill criterion**: the observation, with a number and a date, that would
   make you exit. "Estimates turn down after the November print" is a kill criterion.
   "Thesis breaks" is not.
5. **Pre-mortem.** Assume it is twelve months later and the position lost 40%. Write
   the most likely reason in one sentence. If that reason is already visible in the
   evidence, the verdict changes.
6. **Red-team.** Hand the top names to an adversarial subagent with `redteam-brief.md`.
   It argues the bear case from the same records and must name the single strongest
   fact against the verdict. Answer it in the "What can break it" cell.
7. **Size.** Expected return over bear-case loss, scaled by the mandate. A name that
   fits the mandate's risk gets core size; a name with a binary event or top-decile
   volatility gets sleeve size with the kill date written down.
8. **Register the verdict** in the ledger with price, date, horizon, scenario tree and
   kill criterion, so the next probe can score this one. A probe that never checks its
   own record cannot improve.

## 5. Calibration

Before writing, read `data/ledger/verdicts.jsonl` for every earlier verdict on the
same ticker. State whether it was right so far and why. Across all past verdicts, note
the hit rate by tier and whether the scenario probabilities were too confident. Adjust
this probe's probabilities toward the record. Write the calibration note into the
"What this probe cannot tell you" section.
